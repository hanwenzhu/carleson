10 Two-sided Metric Space Carleson

We prove a variant of Theorem 1.1.1 for a two-sided Calderón–Zygmund kernel on the doubling metric measure space \((X,\rho ,\mu ,a)\), i.e. a one-sided Calderón–Zygmund kernel \(K\) which additionally satisfies for all \(x,x',y\in X\) with \(x\neq y\) and \(2\rho (x,x') \leq \rho (x,y)\),

\begin{equation} \label{eqkernel-x-smooth} |K(x,y) - K(x',y)| \leq \left(\frac{\rho (x,x')}{\rho (x,y)}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\frac{2^{a^3}}{V(x,y)}. \end{equation}
10.0.1

By the additional regularity, we can weaken the assumption 1.1.15 to a family of operators that is easier to work with in applications. Namely, for \(r {\gt} 0\), \(x\in X\), and a bounded, measurable function \(f:X\to {\mathbb {C}}\) supported on a set of finite measure, we define

\begin{equation} \label{def-T-r} T_r f(x):= \int _{r\le \rho (x,y)} K(x,y) f(y) \, d\mu (y) = \int _{X\setminus B(x,r)} K(x,y) f(y) \, d\mu (y). \end{equation}
10.0.2

Theorem 10.0.1 two-sided metric space Carleson

For all integers \(a \ge 4\) and real numbers \(1{\lt}q\le 2\) the following holds. Let \((X,\rho ,\mu ,a)\) be a doubling metric measure space. Let \({\Theta }\) be a cancellative compatible collection of functions and let \(K\) be a two-sided Calderón–Zygmund kernel on \((X,\rho ,\mu ,a)\). Assume that for every bounded measurable function \(g\) on \(X\) supported on a set of finite measure and all \(r{\gt}0\) we have

\begin{equation} \label{two-sided-Hr-bound-assumption} \| T_r g\| _{2} \leq 2^{a^3} \| g\| _2\, . \end{equation}
10.0.3

Then for all Borel sets \(F\) and \(G\) in \(X\) and all Borel functions \(f:X\to {\mathbb {C}}\) with \(|f|\le \mathbf{1}_F\), we have, with \(T\) defined in 1.1.14,

\begin{equation} \label{two-sided-resweak} \left|\int _{G} T f \, \mathrm{d}\mu \right| \leq \frac{2^{474a^3}}{(q-1)^6} \mu (G)^{1-\frac{1}{q}} \mu (F)^{\frac{1}{q}}\, . \end{equation}
10.0.4

Proof ▶

Let \(1{\lt}q\le 2\) be a real number. Let \(\Theta \) be a cancellative compatible collection of functions. By the assumption 10.0.3, we can apply 10.0.2 to obtain for every bounded measurable \(g:X\to {\mathbb {C}}\) supported on a set of finite measure,

\begin{equation} \label{original-operator-assumption} \| T_*g\| _2\le 2^{3a^3}\| g\| _2. \end{equation}
10.0.5

Define

\begin{equation*} K’(x,y):= 2^{-2a^3} K(x,y) \, . \end{equation*}

Then \(K'\) is a two-sided Calderón–Zygmund kernel on \((X,\rho ,\mu ,a)\). Denote the corresponding maximally truncated non-tangential singular operator by \(T_*'\) and the corresponding generalized Carleson operator by \(T'\). With 10.0.5, we obtain for \(g\) as above,

\begin{equation} \label{modified-operator-assumption} \| T_*'g\| _2\le 2^{a^3}\| g\| _2. \end{equation}
10.0.6

Applying 1.1.1 for \(K'\) yields that for all Borel sets \(F\) and \(G\) in \(X\) and all Borel functions \(f:X\to {\mathbb {C}}\) with \(|f|\le \mathbf{1}_F\), we have

\begin{equation*} \left|\int _{G} T’ f \, \mathrm{d}\mu \right| \leq \frac{2^{450a^3}}{(q-1)^6} \mu (G)^{1-\frac{1}{q}} \mu (F)^{\frac{1}{q}}\, . \end{equation*}

This finishes the proof since for all \(x\in X\),

\begin{equation*} T’f(x) = 2^{-2a^3} Tf(x) \, . \end{equation*}

For the remainder of this chapter, fix an integer \(a\ge 4\), a doubling metric measure space \((X,\rho ,\mu ,a)\) and a two-sided Calderón–Zygmund kernel \(K\) as in Theorem 10.0.1.

Proof ▶

Proof ▶

By definition,

\begin{equation} \label{xx'difference} \left| T_r g(x) - T_r g(x') \right| =\left|\int _{r\le \rho (x,y)} K(x,y) g(y) \, d\mu (y) -\int _{r\le \rho (x',y)} K(x',y) g(y)\, d\mu (y) \right| \, . \end{equation}

We split the first integral in [xx’difference] into the domains \(r\le \rho (x,y){\lt}2r\) and \(2r\le \rho (x,y)\). The integral over the first domain we estimate by \(\ref{firstxx'}\) below. \begin{equation} \label{firstxx'} \int _{r\le \rho (x,y){\lt} 2r} |K(x,y)| |g(y)| \, d\mu (y) \end{equation}
10.1.2

\begin{equation} \label{secondxx'} + \left|\int _{2r\le \rho (x,y)} (K(x,y)-K(x',y)) g(y) \, d\mu (y) \right| \end{equation}
10.1.3

\begin{equation} \label{thirdxx'} +\int _{r\le \rho (x',y), r\le \rho (x,y){\lt}2r} |K(x',y)| |g(y)|\, d\mu (y)\, . \end{equation}

Using the bound on \(K\) in 1.1.11 and the doubling condition 1.1.2, we estimate [firstxx’] by

\begin{align} \int _{r\le \rho (x,y){\lt}2r} \frac{2^{a^3}}{V(x,y)} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y)\, & \le \frac{2^{a^3}}{\mu (B(x,r))} \int _{r\le \rho (x,y){\lt}2r} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y)\, \\ \label{firstxx'b} & \le \frac{2^{a^3} \cdot 2^a}{\mu (B(x,2r))} \int _{\rho (x,y){\lt}2r} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y)\, . \end{align}

Using the definition of \(Mg\), we estimate [firstxx’b] by

\begin{equation} \label{firstxx'c} \le 2^{a^3 + a} {Mg(x)}\, . \end{equation}

Similarly, in the domain of [thirdxx’] we note by the triangle inequality and assumption on \(x'\) that \(\rho (x',y){\lt}3r\) and thus we estimate [thirdxx’] by

\begin{equation} \label{thirdxx'b} \frac{2^{a^3}}{\mu (B(x',r))} \int _{\rho (x',y){\lt}4r} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y)\le 2^{a^3 + 2a} Mg(x) \end{equation}
10.1.8

We turn to the remaining term. Using 10.0.1, we estimate [secondxx’] by

\begin{equation} \label{secondxx'b} \int _{2r\le \rho (x,y)} \left(\frac{\rho (x,x')}{\rho (x,y)}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\frac{2^{a^3}}{V(x,y)} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y) \end{equation}

We decompose and estimate [secondxx’] with the triangle inequality by

\begin{align} & \sum _{j=1}^\infty \int _{2^jr\le \rho (x,y){\lt} 2^{j+1}r} \left(\frac{\rho (x,x')}{\rho (x,y)}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\frac{2^{a^3}}{V(x,y)} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y)\, \\ \le & \sum _{j=1}^\infty \left( 2^{-j} \right)^{\frac{1}{a}} \int _{2^jr\le \rho (x,y){\lt} 2^{j+1}r} \frac{2^{a^3}}{\mu (B(x,2^j r))} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y) \\ \le & \sum _{j=1}^\infty 2^{-\frac{j}{a}} \frac{2^{a^3 + a}}{\mu (B(x,2^{j+1} r))} \int _{\rho (x,y){\lt}2^{j+1}r} |g(y)| \, d\mu (y) \\ \label{secondxx'c} \le & 2^{a^3 + a} \sum _{j=1}^\infty 2^{-\frac{j}{a}} Mg(x)\, . \end{align}

\begin{equation} \label{secondxx'd} \le 2^{a^3 + 2a} Mg(x) \, . \end{equation}
10.1.14

Summing the estimates for [firstxx’], [secondxx’], and [thirdxx’] proves the lemma.

Lemma 10.1.3

Proof ▶

Let \(x\) and \(x'\) be given with \(\rho (x,x')\le \frac{R}{4}\). By 10.1.2, we estimate the left-hand-side of 10.1.15 by

\begin{equation} \label{eqcotlar0} |T_R(g)(x')|+ 2^{a^3 + 2a + 2} Mg(x)\, . \end{equation}

We have

\begin{equation} \label{eqcotlar-1} T_R(g)(x')= \int _{R\le \rho (x',y)} K(x',y) g(y) \, d\mu (y)\, . \end{equation}

On the domain \(R\le \rho (x',y)\), we have \(\frac{R}2\le \rho (x,y)\). Hence we may write for 10.1.17

\begin{equation*} T_R(g)(x’)=\int _{R\le \rho (x',y)} K(x’,y) (g-g\mathbf{1}_{B(x,\frac{R}2)})(y) \, d\mu (y) \end{equation*}

We have

\begin{equation*} (T_r-T_R)(g-g\mathbf{1}_{B(x,\frac{R}2)})(x’) \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} = \int _{B(x',R)\setminus B(x',r)} K(x’,y) (g-g\mathbf{1}_{B(x,\frac{R}2)})(y) \, d\mu (y) \end{equation*}

\begin{equation} \label{eqcotlar2} = \int _{B(x',R)\setminus (B(x',r) \cup B(x,\frac{R}{2}))} K(x',y) g(y) \, d\mu (y) \end{equation}

By the triangle inequality, 10.1.15 follows now from 10.1.19 and the estimate for 10.1.20.
